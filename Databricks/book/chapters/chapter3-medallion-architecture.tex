\section{Week 3 Roadmap and Mental Model}
Week 2 gave you a trustworthy table format; Week 3 organizes many tables into a trustworthy \emph{system}. The medallion architecture is the standard answer to a recurring failure: pipelines that transform raw data directly into reports, so that every bug fix requires either corrupting the report layer or re-pulling from the source \cite{databricksMedallion}.

The mental model: \textbf{each layer has one job and one audience}. Bronze answers ``what exactly did we receive?'' for engineers. Silver answers ``what is the clean, current truth?'' for pipelines. Gold answers ``what does the business measure?'' for analysts and models. A row may restate many times in Gold, but it lands in Bronze exactly once.

\begin{definitionbox}
\textbf{The medallion architecture} is a lakehouse design pattern that progressively refines data through Bronze (raw, append-only), Silver (validated, conformed, deduplicated), and Gold (business-level, dimensional, consumable) layers, with quality gates and reconciliation at each transition.
\end{definitionbox}

\begin{keyterms}
\textbf{Quarantine}: a table holding rejected rows with rejection reasons. \textbf{Conformance}: aligning schemas, units, and reference data across sources. \textbf{Reconciliation}: the arithmetic proof that no rows were lost between layers. \textbf{Idempotent transition}: re-running a layer's pipeline yields the same layer state. \textbf{Star schema}: Gold's fact-plus-dimensions shape.
\end{keyterms}

\section{Monday: Why Layers, and the Cost of Skipping Them}
\subsection{Progressive Refinement}
Each transition does exactly one class of work. Bronze $\rightarrow$ Silver: parsing, typing, validation, deduplication. Silver $\rightarrow$ Gold: business logic, conformance across domains, dimensional modeling. Mixing classes---business logic in Silver, typing in Gold---creates pipelines that cannot be rerun, debugged, or audited independently.

\subsection{Idempotent Reprocessing}
The layering pays off on the bad day: a Silver bug is fixed by reprocessing Silver from Bronze; a Gold business-rule change is fixed by rebuilding Gold from Silver. Neither touches the source. This only works if transitions are idempotent---key-based \texttt{MERGE} or partition-scoped \texttt{replaceWhere}---and if Bronze retains everything needed to rebuild.

\begin{pitfall}
\textbf{Transforming in place.} Updating Bronze rows during cleansing destroys the evidence layer. Bronze is write-once; corrections and reinterpretations belong to downstream layers. If storage cost tempts you to mutate Bronze, remember what you are trading: the ability to ever rebuild.
\end{pitfall}

\section{Tuesday: Bronze---The Evidence Layer}
\subsection{Append-Only Raw Capture}
Bronze tables store what arrived, as it arrived, plus metadata your future self will need: \texttt{\_ingested\_at}, \texttt{\_source\_file}, \texttt{\_source\_system}, and a pipeline run identifier. Types stay permissive (strings are common) because Bronze's job is capture, not judgment.

\begin{lstlisting}[language=Python,caption={Bronze landing with ingestion metadata.},label={lst:ch3-bronze}]
from pyspark.sql import functions as F

raw = (spark.read.option("header", True)
       .csv("/Volumes/de_training/bronze/landing/olist/orders/"))

bronze = (raw
    .withColumn("_ingested_at", F.current_timestamp())
    .withColumn("_source_file", F.input_file_name())
    .withColumn("_source_system", F.lit("olist"))
    .withColumn("_run_id", F.lit(spark.conf.get("spark.databricks.job.id", "manual"))))

(bronze.write.format("delta").mode("append")
   .saveAsTable("de_training.bronze.orders"))
\end{lstlisting}

\subsection{Schema-on-Read Trade-offs}
Keeping Bronze loosely typed means a malformed column never blocks ingestion---the failure surfaces in Silver where it can be quarantined and explained. The trade-off is discipline: Silver must parse defensively, and someone must own watching the quarantine.

\section{Wednesday: Silver Conformance and the Gold Star Schema}
\subsection{Silver: Validation, Dedup, Quarantine}
Silver is where data earns trust: typed columns, enforced constraints, deduplicated business keys, and a quarantine table catching what fails with a reason attached.

\begin{lstlisting}[language=Python,caption={Silver cleansing with quarantine accounting.},label={lst:ch3-silver}]
bronze = spark.read.table("de_training.bronze.orders")

typed = (bronze
    .withColumn("order_ts", F.to_timestamp("order_purchase_timestamp"))
    .withColumn("total_amount", F.col("payment_value").cast("decimal(12,2)")))

valid = typed.where("order_id IS NOT NULL AND total_amount >= 0")
rejected = (typed.where("order_id IS NULL OR total_amount < 0")
                 .withColumn("_reject_reason", F.lit("null key or negative amount")))

# Idempotent upsert into Silver; rejected rows are never dropped silently
(valid.write.format("delta").mode("append").saveAsTable("staging.orders_valid"))
spark.sql("""MERGE INTO de_training.silver.orders t
             USING staging.orders_valid s ON t.order_id = s.order_id
             WHEN NOT MATCHED THEN INSERT *""")
(rejected.write.format("delta").mode("append")
   .saveAsTable("de_training.silver.orders_quarantine"))
\end{lstlisting}

\subsection{Reconciliation}
Every transition publishes its arithmetic: \texttt{bronze\_in = silver\_accepted + quarantined}. A one-line assertion in the pipeline (and later, a DLT expectation or a Workflow gate) turns silent row loss into a loud failure.

\subsection{Gold: The Dimensional Contract}
Gold is modeled for consumption: \texttt{fact\_orders} (measures, foreign keys) surrounded by \texttt{dim\_customer}, \texttt{dim\_product}, \texttt{dim\_date}. Facts are narrow and additive; dimensions are conformed and slowly changing where history matters. Gold is the only layer analysts should query by default---Chapter 8 enforces that with grants.

\section{Thursday Hands-on Project: The Olist Medallion}
Build the three-layer flow for the Olist e-commerce dataset \cite{olistDataset}: land nine raw CSVs into Bronze, cleanse into Silver with quarantine, publish a Gold star schema, and reconcile every transition.
\index{hands-on lab}\index{medallion architecture!lab}\index{quarantine!lab}

\begin{lstlisting}[language=Python,caption={Gold star schema build with reconciliation.},label={lst:ch3-gold}]
from pyspark.sql import functions as F

orders = spark.read.table("de_training.silver.orders")
items  = spark.read.table("de_training.silver.order_items")
cust   = spark.read.table("de_training.silver.customers")

dim_customer = cust.select("customer_id", "customer_unique_id",
                           "customer_city", "customer_state")
dim_customer.write.format("delta").mode("overwrite") \
    .saveAsTable("de_training.gold.dim_customer")

fact_orders = (orders.join(items, "order_id")
    .groupBy("order_id", "customer_id", F.to_date("order_ts").alias("order_date"))
    .agg(F.sum("price").alias("net_amount"),
         F.count("order_item_id").alias("line_count")))
fact_orders.write.format("delta").mode("overwrite") \
    .saveAsTable("de_training.gold.fact_orders")

# Reconciliation: Gold facts must equal Silver order count
silver_n = spark.table("de_training.silver.orders").count()
gold_n   = spark.table("de_training.gold.fact_orders").count()
assert silver_n == gold_n, f"reconciliation failed: {silver_n} != {gold_n}"
\end{lstlisting}

\subsection*{Deliverables}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Bronze tables for all nine Olist CSVs with ingestion metadata columns.
    \item Silver tables with typed schemas, deduplicated keys, and populated quarantine tables.
    \item Gold \texttt{fact\_orders}, \texttt{dim\_customer}, \texttt{dim\_product}, \texttt{dim\_date}.
    \item A reconciliation notebook asserting accepted + rejected = input for every table.
\end{itemize}

\subsection*{Acceptance Criteria}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Full re-run from a clean clone changes zero Silver/Gold row counts (idempotence proof).
    \item At least one deliberately poisoned CSV lands in quarantine with a reason, and reconciliation still balances.
    \item Gold aggregates (total revenue) match a direct Bronze computation within rounding.
    \item Lineage columns (\texttt{\_source\_file}, \texttt{\_ingested\_at}) trace a Gold row to its raw file.
\end{itemize}

\subsection*{Stretch Goals}
\begin{itemize}

    \item Convert one Silver transition to \texttt{replaceWhere} partition overwrite and explain when it beats MERGE.
\end{itemize}

\section{Friday Use Case: Two Years of Reprocessing Without Double-Counting}
A retail chain changes its revenue recognition rule: cancelled-then-refunded orders must now net against the month of the original sale. Two years of Gold must be rebuilt. The CFO's constraint: at no point may the revenue number double-count, and the old numbers must remain queryable for the restatement filing.
\index{reprocessing}\index{restatement}\index{retail}

\subsection{Requirements and Assumptions}
Bronze retains all raw extracts (append-only). Gold serves dashboards that finance reads daily. The restatement window is one weekend. Fifty downstream reports reference \texttt{gold.fact\_orders} by name.

\subsection{Architecture Decisions}
\textbf{Parallel build, atomic swap.} Silver and Gold v2 build alongside v1 from immutable Bronze. Reconciliation gates the cutover: v2 totals are compared against v1 with an expected-delta report derived from the rule change. Cutover is a view re-point (\texttt{CREATE OR REPLACE VIEW gold.fact\_orders AS SELECT * FROM gold\_v2.fact\_orders})---one commit, one history entry, instantly reversible.

\textbf{Evidence.} The old tables persist under \texttt{gold\_v1\_archive} for the filing; \texttt{DESCRIBE HISTORY} plus the reconciliation report form the restatement evidence pack.

\begin{pitfall}
\textbf{Backfilling in place during business hours.} Rewriting \texttt{gold.fact\_orders} while dashboards read it produces mixed-rule numbers---the worst outcome: not wrong enough to notice, not right enough to file. Parallel build plus atomic swap exists precisely for this scenario.
\end{pitfall}

\section{Operational Guidance for Week 3}
\index{operational guidance!Week 3}%
\subsection{Performance and Reliability}
The reconciliation ledger is the layer's heartbeat: every transition appends its arithmetic (input, accepted, quarantined) and a daily report trends it---a rising quarantine rate is a source-schema change announcing itself before anyone calls. Reprocessing drills are scheduled, not aspirational: once a month, rebuild one Silver table from Bronze in a scratch schema and diff it. Gold rebuild cost should be measured and documented so restatement requests come with a known price.

\subsection{Security and Governance Checklist}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Layer grants match the audience model: pipelines write Bronze, engineers write Silver, analysts read Gold.
    \item Quarantine tables inherit the source's PII classification---rejected rows still contain PII.
    \item Bronze retention set from the reprocessing objective, not storage cost alone.
    \item Lineage metadata columns (\texttt{\_source\_file}, \texttt{\_ingested\_at}) populated on every Bronze write.
\end{itemize}

\section{Interview Q\&A}
\subsection{Concepts and Trade-offs}
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Q: Why keep Bronze forever when storage costs money?}\\
    A: Bronze is the only layer that can answer ``what did the source actually say'' and the only input for rebuilding after logic changes. Its cost is small next to re-acquisition or restatement failure.
    \item \textbf{Q: When is two layers (skip Silver) acceptable?}\\
    A: Almost never for shared data. A single-source, single-consumer pipeline may collapse layers, but the moment a second consumer or a reprocessing need appears, you rebuild Silver under pressure.
    \item \textbf{Q: How do you prove a transition lost no rows?}\\
    A: Reconciliation arithmetic per run: input count equals accepted plus quarantined, asserted in the pipeline and logged to a run ledger.
    \item \textbf{Q: MERGE or \texttt{replaceWhere} for Silver?}\\
    A: MERGE for keyed upserts arriving as changes; \texttt{replaceWhere} for full partition restatements (daily snapshots). Both are idempotent; mixing them per table is not.
    \item \textbf{Q: Why a star schema in Gold rather than one wide table?}\\
    A: Shared conformed dimensions let many facts join consistently, selective scans stay cheap, and BI tools model relationships natively. Wide tables duplicate dimension logic into every fact.
    \item \textbf{Q: Who owns the quarantine table?}\\
    A: A named steward with a review cadence. Quarantine without an owner is where data goes to be forgotten; with one, it is your earliest upstream-quality signal.
    \item \textbf{Q: Why does the medallion make streaming easier later?}\\
    A: Each layer transition already has idempotent, keyed write semantics---the exact properties Structured Streaming sinks require. A well-built batch medallion becomes a streaming medallion by changing triggers, not contracts.
    \item \textbf{Q: Where do source-system reference tables (country codes, calendars) live?}\\
    A: As governed Gold dimensions with an owner and a refresh contract---they are conformed shared assets, and treating them as casual lookups is how three definitions of ``fiscal month'' happen.
\end{enumerate}

\section*{Key Takeaways}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Bronze is evidence, Silver is truth, Gold is measurement---one job and one audience per layer.
    \item Every transition is idempotent (keyed MERGE or partition overwrite) and reconciled by arithmetic.
    \item Quarantine tables turn silent data loss into visible, explainable rejections.
    \item Reprocessing is a first-class design requirement: build v2 in parallel, gate on reconciliation, swap atomically.
    \item Catalog/schema naming (\texttt{catalog.bronze|silver|gold.table}) is governance infrastructure, not cosmetics.
\end{itemize}
